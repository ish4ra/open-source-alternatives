# Hidden gems and underrated picks

These are open-source projects that are easy to miss outside their niche. This is **not** a popularity ranking: some are already well known inside FOSS/self-hosting communities, but still much less visible than the proprietary tools they can replace.

> Projects here should still meet the same maintenance and usefulness standards as the main directory. Experimental/beta software is marked clearly.

## Browsing and privacy

### [Helium](https://helium.computer/)

A privacy-focused Chromium browser based on ungoogled-chromium, with an intentionally minimal UI and built-in blocking. Helium keeps its browser components and tooling open source.

**Why it stands out:** a clean Chromium experience without the feature/telemetry baggage many people dislike in larger browsers.

**Caveat:** Helium is still a relatively young **beta** project with a small team. That is fine for enthusiasts, but maturity and security-update trust should be part of your threat-model decision for sensitive workflows.

## Authentication and security

### [Ente Auth](https://ente.io/auth/)

A free open-source 2FA authenticator with end-to-end encrypted backups, multi-device synchronization, offline token generation, and import/export support.

**Why it stands out:** it combines the convenience people liked about cloud-synced authenticators with open-source clients and encrypted backups.

### [Aegis Authenticator](https://getaegis.app/)

An Android authenticator with an encrypted local vault and strong backup/import controls.

**Why it stands out:** excellent when you want an offline-first authenticator without depending on an account or cloud service.

## File transfer

### [PairDrop](https://pairdrop.net/)

AirDrop-style browser file sharing that works across platforms. It supports local peer-to-peer transfers, pairing/public rooms, and self-hosting.

**Why it stands out:** one of the easiest ways to move a file between Android, iOS, Windows, macOS, and Linux without installing a platform-specific transfer app.

## Notes and productivity

### [Rnote](https://rnote.flxzt.net/)

A vector-based handwriting and sketching app with stylus support, an infinite canvas, PDF/image annotation, and cross-platform builds.

**Why it stands out:** a strong open-source option for people who want a tablet-style handwritten notebook rather than another Markdown editor.

### [Super Productivity](https://super-productivity.com/)

An open-source task manager with time tracking, planning/timeboxing, and developer-oriented integrations.

**Why it stands out:** unusually feature-rich for a local productivity app and useful for people who want tasks and time tracking in one place.

### [Memos](https://usememos.com/)

A lightweight self-hosted Markdown note system built around fast timeline-style capture instead of complex folder structures.

**Why it stands out:** very little friction between having a thought and saving it, while keeping the data on infrastructure you control.

## Bookmarks and web archiving

### [Karakeep](https://karakeep.app/)

A self-hostable “bookmark everything” app for links, notes, and images with full-text search and optional automatic tagging.

**Why it stands out:** closer to a personal searchable web inbox than a traditional bookmark folder tree.

### [Linkwarden](https://linkwarden.app/)

A self-hosted collaborative bookmark/read-it-later tool that can preserve webpages as screenshots and PDFs.

**Why it stands out:** useful links remain readable even when the original page disappears or changes.

## Self-hosted infrastructure

### [Gatus](https://github.com/TwiN/gatus)

A lightweight status/uptime monitor configured primarily through YAML, with alerting and status-page support.

**Why it stands out:** particularly nice for GitOps/config-as-code users who do not want to configure every monitor by clicking through a UI.

### [Glance](https://github.com/glanceapp/glance)

A self-hosted dashboard that can combine feeds such as RSS, video/community updates, monitoring data, and other widgets on one page.

**Why it stands out:** a focused personal homepage without turning into a full portal platform.

## Media

### [Yamtrack](https://github.com/FuzzyGrim/Yamtrack)

A self-hosted tracker for movies, TV, anime, manga, games, books, comics, and board games. It can integrate with Jellyfin, Plex, and Emby and import from several tracking services.

**Why it stands out:** a self-hosted alternative to spreading your watch/read/play history across several commercial tracking sites.

### [Ampcast](https://github.com/rekkyrosso/ampcast)

A Winamp-inspired open-source music player that can connect to Jellyfin, Navidrome/Subsonic, Plex, Emby and several online music sources. It is available as a PWA, desktop app, and self-hosted web app.

**Why it stands out:** one interface can sit in front of multiple music libraries/services, with visualizers, scrobbling, lyrics and gapless playback.

## Suggest a hidden gem

A good candidate should be genuinely useful, actively maintained, clearly licensed, and offer something meaningfully different from the obvious mainstream recommendation. A small project is welcome; an abandoned project is not.
